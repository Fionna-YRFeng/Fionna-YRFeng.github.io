ABOUT ME

Building agents that
remember and improve.

I am a master's student in Data Science at UC San Diego and a research intern in the AI Memory Systems Group at Microsoft Research Asia, where I work with Qianxi Zhang and Qi Chen. Previously, I received my B.S. in Data Science and Big Data Technology from Southern University of Science and Technology (SUSTech).

My research asks how we can build autonomous agents that learn from experience and remain reliable over long horizons. I approach this through two connected problems: memory, which must preserve the dependencies and validity of an evolving execution state, and self-evolution, which must turn sparse, ambiguous feedback into meaningful changes in behavior. My work explores structured memory, task-adaptive evidence selection, and hypothesis-driven exploration to help agents diagnose failures, test alternatives, and retain useful progress. I am particularly interested in the interaction between reasoning, memory, and learning: how an agent decides what to recall, what to question, and what to change as a task unfolds. My long-term goal is to develop agents that can continually adapt through interaction while keeping their decisions grounded in evidence and their computational costs manageable.
